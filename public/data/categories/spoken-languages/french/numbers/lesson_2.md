# Counting Higher: Numbers 11-20

## From 10 to 20: A New Pattern 🔢

In the previous lesson, you mastered numbers 0-10. Now it's time to build on that foundation! Numbers 11-20 follow a logical pattern, though they introduce some new words you'll need to memorize.

> **Key Insight**: Unlike English, French numbers 11-19 don't have a simple "-teen" suffix. Instead, they combine unique names with the base "dix" (10).

## Understanding the Pattern

### The "Special Teens" (11-16)

Numbers 11-16 are special in French—they don't follow the simple "10 + number" pattern like English. Instead, they have unique names:

- **11 = onze** (not "dix-un")
- **12 = douze** (not "dix-deux")
- **13 = treize** (not "dix-trois")
- **14 = quatorze** (not "dix-quatre")
- **15 = quinze** (not "dix-cinq")
- **16 = seize** (not "dix-six")

**Important**: These must be memorized as standalone words.

### The "Pattern Teens" (17-19)

Starting with 17, the logical pattern emerges:

- **17 = dix-sept** (10 + 7)
- **18 = dix-huit** (10 + 8)
- **19 = dix-neuf** (10 + 9)

From here on out, French follows this additive pattern consistently!

### Twenty: The New Base

- **20 = vingt** (van)

The number 20 is its own base, completely different from the "dix" (10) series. Remember this—it will make numbers 21-99 much easier to understand later.

## Pronunciation Deep Dive

### The "Z" Sound in Teens

Notice how many of these numbers end with a "z" sound:

- **douze** (12) - ends with "z"
- **treize** (13) - ends with "z"
- **quatorze** (14) - ends with "z"
- **quinze** (15) - ends with "z"
- **seize** (16) - ends with "z"

This "z" sound is crucial for distinguishing these numbers when speaking. Practice saying them quickly!

### The Silent Letters Return

Remember the silent letters from Lesson 1?

- **vingt** (20) - the final "t" is sometimes silent, sometimes pronounced (it's pronounced when used alone, like "20 degrees")
- **dix-sept** (17) - the "x" in "dix" becomes a "z" sound before "sept"

## Hearing Numbers in Sequence

Let's practice saying sequences. Say these out loud:

```
11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16
↓
onze, douze, treize, quatorze, quinze, seize

17, 18, 19, 20
↓
dix-sept, dix-huit, dix-neuf, vingt
```

Try to speed up as you get comfortable!

## Real-World Contexts

### Using Numbers 11-20 in Sentences

- "Je suis né le douze décembre" (I was born on December 12th)
- "J'ai quinze ans" (I'm 15 years old)
- "C'est dix-sept euros" (It's 17 euros)
- "Numéro dix-neuf" (Number 19)
- "Il y a vingt personnes" (There are 20 people)

### Common Phrases

- **"Excusez-moi, c'est combien?"** (How much is this?) - Response might be: "dix-sept euros"
- **"Quel âge as-tu?"** (How old are you?) - Response: "J'ai dix-huit ans"

## Grammar Note: Agreement with "Vingt"

The word "vingt" (20) has a special rule:

- **20** = "vingt" (just the number)
- **21** = "vingt et un" (20 and 1) - note the "et" (and)
- **22** = "vingt-deux" (20-2) - note: NO "et" between 21-29
- **20 dollars** = "vingt dollars" - "vingt" stays singular
- **20 people** = "vingt personnes" - no agreement
- **80** = "quatre-vingts" (4 × 20) - this DOES add an "s" when not followed by another number!
