# Introduction to French Numbers (0-10)

## Welcome to French Numbers! 🎓

Learning to count in French is one of the first steps in your language journey. In this lesson, you'll learn the fundamental numbers from zero to ten—the building blocks for all number-related conversations.

> **Pronunciation Tip**: French numbers follow predictable patterns. The key is to listen carefully to native speakers and mimic the nasal sounds, especially for numbers like "un," "neuf," and "sept."

## The Basics: 0-10

Here are the French numbers from zero to ten with their pronunciation guides:

| Number | French | Pronunciation | Notes |
|--------|--------|----------------|-------|
| 0 | zéro | zay-roh | Same as English "zero" |
| 1 | un | uhn | Masculine form; use "une" for feminine nouns |
| 2 | deux | duh | Similar to English "duh" |
| 3 | trois | twah | The "oi" makes a "wa" sound |
| 4 | quatre | ka-tr | The "qu" sounds like "k" |
| 5 | cinq | sank | Ends with a silent "q" |
| 6 | six | sees | Pronounced "sees" (not "six") |
| 7 | sept | set | Rhymes with English "bet" |
| 8 | huit | wee | Sounds like "we" |
| 9 | neuf | nuhf | The "eu" sound is tricky—practice! |
| 10 | dix | dees | Pronounced "dees" (not "diks") |

## Key Pronunciation Patterns

### The Silent Letter Mystery

In French, many final consonants are silent. Notice how:
- **six** (6) is pronounced "sees" not "siks"
- **dix** (10) is pronounced "dees" not "diks"
- **neuf** (9) changes pronunciation based on the next word

### The Nasal "N" Sound

When "n" appears before certain letters, it creates a nasal sound:
- **un** - the "n" makes a nasalized "uhn" sound
- **cinq** - the "n" creates "sank" (like in English "tank")

Practice these by pinching your nose slightly and letting the sound resonate through your nasal cavity!

## Common Phrases with Numbers

### Using Numbers in Context

Once you know how to say the numbers, it's time to use them:

```
- "J'ai un chat" (I have one cat) - Jay uhn sha
- "Deux personnes" (Two people) - Duh per-sohn
- "C'est trois dollars" (It's three dollars) - Say twah dol-ar
```

### Quick Drill

Try saying these combinations aloud:
- **un, deux, trois** (one, two, three)
- **quatre, cinq, six** (four, five, six)
- **sept, huit, neuf** (seven, eight, nine)
- **dix** (ten)

## Practice Tips 💡

1. **Listen and Repeat**: Use our pronunciation guides, but also listen to native speakers on language learning apps
2. **Write Them Down**: Handwriting helps cement the memory
3. **Use Flash Cards**: Create cards with the numeral on one side and French spelling on the other
4. **Count Out Loud**: Practice counting 0-10 repeatedly until it becomes automatic
5. **Real-World Application**: Start noticing numbers when watching French media or news

## Did You Know? 🌟

The French number system has some interesting quirks:
- After 69, the numbers get creative! 70 is "soixante-dix" (60+10), 80 is "quatre-vingts" (4×20), and 90 is "quatre-vingt-dix" (4×20+10)
- But don't worry—we'll cover those advanced patterns in later lessons!
